# Predictive Maintenance Insights

## Overview

In this project, I analyzed equipment operational data to understand machine failures, risk levels, and maintenance patterns.

Using Python and Power BI, I explored how different factors like torque and tool wear relate to equipment failure and operational risk.

The dashboard helps in identifying high-risk machines and understanding common failure types.


# Main Findings

## 1. Heat Dissipation Failure Occurred Most Frequently

From the analysis, Heat Dissipation Failure had the highest number of failures compared to other failure types.

This may indicate:
- overheating issues,
- cooling inefficiencies,
- or machines operating under heavy load conditions.

### Observation
Machines may require better temperature monitoring and regular cooling system maintenance.

## 2. Most Equipment Falls Under Low Risk

The dashboard shows that most equipment belongs to the low-risk category.

### Observation
This suggests that overall machine conditions are relatively stable.

However, the smaller group of high-risk machines should still be monitored carefully to avoid unexpected breakdowns.

## 3. Higher Tool Wear Shows Higher Risk

While analyzing the scatter plot, I noticed that machines with higher tool wear levels were more likely to appear in higher-risk areas.

### Observation
Tool wear plays an important role in machine reliability and maintenance planning.

Replacing tools before reaching critical wear levels could help reduce failures.


## 4. Type L Equipment Has the Highest Count

The equipment distribution chart shows that Type L machines are present in the highest numbers in the dataset.

### Observation
Since this equipment type is used more frequently, it may also require more regular maintenance monitoring.

## 5. Torque and Tool Wear Show Operational Patterns

The relationship between torque and tool wear shows visible clustering patterns in the scatter plot.

### Observation
Certain operating conditions may contribute to faster equipment wear and increased maintenance needs.

Further analysis could help identify safe operating ranges.
